# Diagnosis: why combos, tutoring and play logic are wrong (2026-09-26)

Eight parallel investigators over the study corpus, code and Forge bytecode; the top 30 of 54 critical/high findings each re-checked by an independent adversarial verifier (4 confirmed, 26 partly with corrected claims, 0 refuted); one synthesis. Evidence scripts are archived off-repo (see tasks/25-repair-plan.md, WS0). The plan built on this is `tasks/25-repair-plan.md`; the UX review is `tasks/26-ux-review.md`.

## In plain words

The AI plays combo decks badly for a few concrete reasons, and none of them is combat or personality, which is where most of the last two months went. First, the combo system gives the AI a shopping list, not instructions: for each deck it passes along every combo Commander Spellbook knows as a bag of card names, and 71% of the 238 cEDH "combos" are engines, like infinite mana or endless triggers, that only win if you also use a finisher the list never names. Second, even when the AI collects every piece, the code treats that as done and hands control back to Forge. The code can only cast spells, never activate abilities or choose targets, so a Kiki-Jiki loop untapped the wrong creature 8 times out of 8, and Derevi untapped Gaea's Cradle 0 times in 216 chances. Of 167 times the AI completed a combo made of permanents, it won that same turn 5 times. Third, Forge's own AI silently refuses to play about one in eight cards in a cEDH deck (cards its authors flagged as "the AI can't play this"), including Demonic Consultation, Vampiric Tutor, Lion's Eye Diamond and Clock of Omens. It refuses them for the opponent seats too, so even the opponents are weaker than their decklists. Tutoring fails in its own ways: tutors are aimed at any combo piece rather than the card that actually wins. The AI's Magda gave up Portal to Phyrexia in all 47 searches where stock Forge would have taken it, and won 15% of games instead of 33%. When the AI decides to tutor for a combo, 44% of the time it picks a tutor that cannot legally find the card it wants, and Forge's own tutor logic waits until the second main phase and usually grabs a creature. The one combo that does work, Thassa's Oracle plus Demonic Consultation, wins around round 5 or 6 like humans do, but it works because of a coding accident, and the same code made the AI exile its own library 14 times. Months of "parity" results hid all of this for two reasons. Most study games used precon decks where the combo code never ran, partly because of a card-name bug. And every change was scored on overall win share, where a gain of about 18 points on Oracle decks and a loss of about 7 on the rest average out to zero. Some study decks were also missing cards: Forge silently dropped 48 double-faced card slots, including the Ral deck's commander, so the conclusion that "Forge can't play storm" was never a fair test. The product then tells users a combo is "AI can fire this; results meaningful" whenever the deck won later by any means, which mostly meant engines that won by combat a round or more later. The order of fixes that follows from the evidence: first fix the measurements (combo conversion rate, per-deck results, a test that starts games with a combo already on the board); then rebuild the combo data so each line names its finisher, order and targets; then add a small step-runner in the shim that plays those steps through Forge's own rules checks; then let the shim re-admit the cards Forge refuses and fix what tutors can reach and when they are cast. Combat tuning should stop until those land. What nobody knows yet is how much win rate a working step-runner buys, and the seeded-board test can answer that cheaply before anything big is built.

## Root causes, ranked

### 1. No combo executor: the shim treats 'assembled' as done, casts only spells from hand, and never makes a loop's activations, target choices or stop decision (confidence: high)

**Symptoms.**
- combos: lines get assembled and then are never fired; the sim wins by combat around round 12-13, while humans win with a loop around round 5
- play_logic: engines sit on the board for 15-20 player-turns; loops stop after 1-30 activations; open-ended loops run to the 900 s clock and are scored as draws
- tutoring: tutored engine pieces produce nothing once found, so tutoring looks useless

**Mechanism.** A line reaches the shim as a set of card names. The shim pursues it only until every piece is owned, then hands control back to stock Forge (PlanPlayerController.java:1068 'assembled, done here'). Its only action is to cast a spell from hand (castableSpell :1251 `if (!sa.isSpell()) continue;`, comboPriority walks only ZoneType.Hand at :1124). It never activates an ability, never picks a target, never casts from the graveyard, and has no stop condition or payoff step. Stock Forge scores each ability and target on its own, with no idea that the pieces form a loop. Forge does activate these cards, but aimlessly: loops that need the same target choice on every pass never start, and repeat loops stop once one more activation looks locally worthless. Only lines that finish by casting a spell (Consultation into Oracle) convert, because there the shim fires the last piece itself.

**Evidence.**
- Code: PlanPlayerController.java:1068, :1124, :1251; DeckPlan.java:208-213 reads only 'cards'. No public decision hook for targets is overridden (the shim overrides 9 of about 120 PlayerControllerAi methods).
- Line-completing casts across 6 plan-seat run sets: 167 permanent completions led to 5 same-turn wins (3.0%) and a 25.7% overall win rate (the 4-seat baseline); 49 spell completions led to 25 wins, 24 of them the same turn (verify/conv).
- Only 18 of 235 deck-line pairs (10 of 122 unique piece sets) can be driven by casting spells from hand. 142/235 need a non-mana activated ability, 176 need an outlet, 126 carry a prerequisite (verify/lines_exec).
- Wrong target on every pass: Kiki-Jiki copied Zealous Conscripts 8 times and the copy's trigger untapped Kiki 0/8 (it untapped Rionya, Mirage Phalanx or Bartz instead). After Gaea's Cradle was tapped, an Emiel blink of Derevi untapped Cradle 0/216 times. Bytecode: ControlGainAi picks opponents' permanents; TapOrUntapAi reuses tap-the-opponent logic.
- Aimless loops (seat-turns with 5 or more activations, then same-turn wins): Emiel 1/37, Staff of Domination 2/17, Clock of Omens 4/27, Brain Freeze recast 3+ times 0/62. Where the byproduct is combat, loops do finish: Godo+Helm 59 same-turn wins (28/32 once Helm is actually on Godo), Magda treasure engine 35/70.
- Funnel on the 3 agent_viability arms: plan seats assemble 191/384 (49.7%) against stock 236/1152 (20.5%), yet win share is 21.9% vs 22.6%. Genuine executions are about 25-32 of 191.
- Humans: 10 of 12 video wins were loops executed on the turn the last piece landed; human mean win round 5.0 against stock 12.7 (recomputed in winner-own-turn units).
- Convert-on-assembly upper bound for agent093: mean win round 13.1 -> 8.8 over all lines, or 10.4 counting kill-clause lines only. Assembly speed is the other half of the gap.

**Already tried.** Stage 5 (54eb2f6, 2026-08-01) designed pursuit up to assembly and then 'hand back to stock'. A18/A19 (02e4451, 08-24) made the tutor gate and exempted loop triggers from the triggerMiss roll. 0.9.3 (37a08a3, 08-29) added the piece-casting veto. Conversion was named the binding gap on 08-24 (human_ceiling RESULTS), 08-26 (agent_viability RESULTS, commit 6171ef4 'conversion becomes the wall'). No conversion task was ever written; 0 of 23 shim commits execute a line. Forge's lookahead was never used in a study. The one scratch experiment in this investigation (setUseSimulation on, 2 games) crashed once (GameCopier 'Couldn't map Goblin Token') and timed out once (585 of 915 s inside simulation, one decision took 361 s).

**Leverage.** Thin shim mechanism plus data. Python compiles each executable line into a step script: ordered actions (cast X, activate Y targeting Z, repeat until N or a condition, then the outlet W), zones, prerequisites, stop condition. The shim interprets it with public Forge APIs. Activations: return the named non-mana SpellAbility from chooseSpellAbilityToPlay after canPlay() and ComputerUtilCost.canPayCost(); activation targets can be set in the same override. Stack-trigger targets: chooseTargetsFor is reached only when the ability has a TargetingPlayer param, so the hook is orderAndPlaySimultaneousSa (set targets, then ComputerUtil.playStack). That replaces super rather than calling it first, so it needs a thin-adapter review. Iteration cap: decline the optional loop trigger in confirmTrigger once the cap is reached. Forge public API: GameSimulator.simulateSpellAbility as a bounded, try/catch-wrapped check that a planned line finishes. Not as the pilot (2/2 experimental games failed).

**Unknowns.** No thin interpreter has been prototyped, so how much conversion or win rate it buys is unmeasured. Open questions: whether overriding orderAndPlaySimultaneousSa reliably captures loop triggers and still counts as a thin adapter; whether Forge's per-API sub-choosers behave once a SpellAbility is forced; how reliably free-English Spellbook descriptions compile into structured steps; CPU cost per turn. Also, stock opponents leave 61-69% of assembled lines untouched for a round (unverified), so fixing conversion alone may make the sim overrate combo decks.

### 2. The plan ships Spellbook's whole catalogue as flat card sets: no win-versus-engine split, no named outlet, no order or zone, no executability filter (confidence: high)

**Symptoms.**
- combos: the AI chases engines (infinite mana, ETB, blink) as if they were kills, and 7 of 32 cEDH decks carry no win line at all
- tutoring: about 70% of combo casts and tutor targets go to engine-only pieces; the outlets that would finish the engines are valued at 1 and never fetched
- combos: human win lines are missing their finisher (Derevi needs Nadu plus Blind Obedience; Sisay needs Deadpool; Cabbage needs Walking Ballista)

**Mechanism.** combos._slim keeps card names, produces and some text, and throws away zoneLocations, template requirements and easyPrerequisites. deck_plan.py:295-301 then turns every 'included' Spellbook variant into a line: sorted by piece count, with no cap, no dedupe and no filter on what it produces. The shim reads only the names (DeckPlan.java:208), so an infinite-mana engine and a real kill look identical. Spellbook expects the player to pair an engine with a payoff. Nothing in the plan does that pairing, and the payoff card in the deck gets search value 1.

**Evidence.**
- Code: deck_plan.py:295-301; combos.py:90-101; DeckPlan.java:208-213 ('produces' appears only in a comment at :28).
- Shipped plans (runs_agent_shipping/plans): 168 of 238 lines (71%) produce no win under a lenient definition (which counts infinite combat, hasty tokens and opponent mill). Only 17/238 literally say 'Win the game', and 16 of those are Oracle + Consultation/Pact/Hermit Druid. 7/32 decks have no win line: derevi, nadu, dexter_kinnan, isaac_yisan, matt_sisay, kinnan, natalie_magda.
- Of 173 'non-win' lines, 111 are one outlet short and that outlet is already in the deck. Thrasios, Finale of Devastation, Walking Ballista and Grapeshot sit at search value 1, and tutor_steer picked them 0 times in about 2,100 plan searches.
- Effort goes to engines: 276/385 combo_casts, 288/426 tutor_cast targets and 529/866 tutor_steer picks hit pieces that appear only in resource lines (pooled over 4 arms).
- Assembled engine lines win at baseline: 36/143 plan seat-games (25%). Assembled win or combat lines: 29/58 (50%). Nothing assembled: 47/311 (15%). Games ran a median of 15.5-20 player-turns after the first engine line assembled.
- Dead lines kept: Tainted Pact + Oracle is in 8 decks, but Tainted Pact exiled at most 1 card in 168 of 168 resolutions. isaac_yisan ships 29 lines; there are 3 exact duplicates.
- Human acceptance check: of 11 human combo wins, 5 are present as lines. 3 clearly lack an outlet the deck contains. The rest fail on decklist fidelity (Rog/Thras is missing Six; Kinnan is the wrong build) or have no label (Magda g1).

**Already tried.** engine/combos.py has one commit ever (0834ece, 2026-07-31); the line source has never been revised. For precons, a 'synergy lines' fallback was built on the false 'zero variants' premise. It made prediction worse (rho 0.255 -> 0.142) and was not shipped. No produces filter has ever existed.

**Leverage.** Data only (Python, deck_plan.py/combos.py). Keep the full Spellbook variant. Classify each variant as win, engine or lock, treating infinite combat phases, infinite hasty tokens and infinite power as wins. Compose engine lines with an outlet already in the deck, and give outlets real search value. Emit order, zone and a final-piece flag per piece, plus prerequisites and a stop condition. Drop or mark Tainted Pact + Oracle and RemoveDeck-blocked lines. Cap and dedupe lines. Use the labelled human win lines as the acceptance set. Hazard: under today's shim, adding Thassa's Oracle as a line piece would get it cast early and 'assembled'. Outlet data must ship together with sequencing and the executor (RC1, RC7).

**Unknowns.** No simulation measured the win-rate effect of rebuilt lines. The 71% figure depends on the classifier (71% lenient, 93% strict). The outlet list is hand-picked (126/168 or 107/168 matched by type; the investigator's 151 was not reproducible). Whether Spellbook's feature/template graph supports automatic outlet composition is unchecked.

### 3. Forge's AI hard-excludes every card scripted AI:RemoveDeck:All, which blocks cEDH combo pieces, some tutors and about 12% of cEDH nonland cards, for opponents too (confidence: high)

**Symptoms.**
- play_logic: stock seats hold about 2.3 dead cards per cEDH game; about 3 nonland cards per precon deck are never cast (Signets, Beast Within, Toxic Deluge); one precon commander (Winter, Cynical Opportunist) is never cast
- combos: engines built on flagged activated abilities (printed Clock of Omens, Devoted Druid, Quirion/Scryb Ranger, Aphetto Alchemist) are never activated by any seat; stock never casts Demonic Consultation
- tutoring: stock never casts Vampiric Tutor, Summoner's Pact, Diabolic Intent
- measurement: stock 'opponents' never cast Consultation, LED, Ad Nauseam, Doomsday or Vampiric, so the cEDH gauntlet is weaker than its decklists

**Mechanism.** AiController.getSpellAbilityToPlay runs saList.removeIf on every spell or ability whose host card's own script carries AI:RemoveDeck:All, and the priority comparator subtracts 10 for them. The removal is unconditional (no profile switch). Only the counterspell pre-pass, ETB counters, PlayBeforeLandDrop and copies of flagged cards escape it. The shim's castableSpell checks only canPlay/canPayCost, so it rescues flagged SPELLS that are in plan lines or tutor lists. It never offers activated abilities, so flagged engines stay dead.

**Evidence.**
- Bytecode (javap, 2.0.13): AiController.getSpellAbilityToPlay offsets 258-264 removeIf(lambda$getSpellAbilityToPlay$8 = isLandAbility() || ComputerUtilCard.isCardRemAIDeck(host)); saComparator offsets 122-128 give -10. canPlaySa and SpellAbilityPicker never read the flag.
- Stock cEDH seats cast 178/3430 (5.2%) of the flagged nonland cards they drew; 175 of those were counterspells cast through the unfiltered pre-pass. Unflagged: 11110/17604 (63.1%). Control group of RemoveDeck:Random cards, which are not filtered: 2727/3914 (69.7%). Precon stock: 24/2570 (0.9%) vs 71.8%.
- Natural experiment with activations: printed flagged permanents 1/459; copies of the same cards 10/16. Printed Clock of Omens 0 activations in 135 battlefield entries, against 344 activations by Sculpting Steel, Metamorph and Impersonator copies.
- Demonic Consultation: 0 stock casts in 69+ seat-games holding it; 51 plan casts through the shim. Thassa's Oracle cast by stock in 138 seat-games, 0 Oracle wins.
- Scope: 91/238 cEDH lines (25/32 decks) contain a flagged piece. 52 are hard-blocked for both seat types (flagged non-mana activation), 10 only for stock (cast-only piece), 29 have only mana abilities flagged. Flagged cards are 12.1% of cEDH nonland cards (285/2362; rograkh_silas 22.1%). Unflagged: Oracle, Tainted Pact, Breach, Kiki-Jiki, Heliod, Ballista, Dramatic Reversal, Portal.
- Plan seats cast 24.6% of flagged cards they hold, and 5.6% on precons (93 of 133 precon flagged casts were incidental instant_hold substitutions).
- Inherited follow-on errors (unverified): 30% of stock classic rituals wasted, with a flagged card in hand in 51/51 reconstructed cases; stock discards line pieces or tutors at cleanup 0.27 times per seat-game; about 10% of precon turns end with a castable permanent unspent, about half of them flagged-only.

**Already tried.** Nothing. No repo file, doc, test or study mentions RemoveDeck or isCardRemAIDeck. The shim's spell rescue is incidental to combo_cast/tutor_cast.

**Leverage.** Thin shim mechanism plus data. After super.chooseSpellAbilityToPlay, collect the plan seat's flagged SpellAbilities that plan data names, including non-mana activated abilities. Evaluate each with the public getAi().canPlaySa() (unfiltered) and take it when it passes and plan timing data allows. Cards flagged because Forge's per-API logic is weak (Consultation NameCard, Ad Nauseam, Final Fortune, Pacts) need timing preconditions as data or they will fire suicidally. Decide explicitly whether opponent seats also get readmission (realistic opponents versus a stock baseline). Do not edit res/ card scripts. Product: list flagged cards per deck (from cardsfolder.zip or Deck.getUnplayableAICards) and keep them out of 'cold' and cut verdicts.

**Unknowns.** Whether readmitted cards are played sensibly: no sim has tested canPlaySa-driven readmission. The win-rate cost of the dead cards (paired A/B needed). Whether flagged mana abilities are usable in payment: LED shows 0 MANA uses. How stock seats activated a Clock 32 times, possibly through the cost-payment path. The ritual-waste link is inferred, not read from ManaAi source.

### 4. Tutor targets are valued by Spellbook membership and keyword regex, not by what this pilot can convert, and combo steering has absolute priority (confidence: medium)

**Symptoms.**
- tutoring: plan Magda discards stock Forge's Portal to Phyrexia pick every time and wins half as often
- tutoring: engines, draw engines and real outlets sit at value 1, so early tutors never fetch an engine (humans do so half the time)
- play_logic: 'loses the game' drawback text scores as a finisher, so plan seats fetch Pact of Negation and lose to their own Pacts and Final Fortunes

**Mechanism.** deck_plan.py:375-393 gives value 8 to any piece of any Spellbook line, and to any card matching the finisher regex ('loses? the game', 'combat damage to a player'). Big bombs get 6; engines and most outlets get 1. In the shim, a sighted line's missing piece wins outright (PlanPlayerController.java:1792), and a plan pick wins whenever its value is strictly higher (:1803). So any engine piece beats a proven finisher, even when Forge cannot run the line the piece belongs to.

**Evidence.**
- Magda, 3 with-lines agent_viability arms: stock picked Portal and the shim overrode it 47/47 times (33 combo-mode, 14 plan-mode); 68/68 across 5 arms. The nocombo arm, with no lines, still overrode 12/12 through plan-mode values 8 over 6.
- Plan-piloted Magda 7/48 (15%) vs stock-piloted Magda 47/144 (33%), Fisher p=0.016, lower in all 3 arms. The gap sits in games where Magda's tutor fired: 5/28 vs 43/79 (p=0.0009). Plan Magda fetched Portal from the library 0 times in 72 games.
- Portal on the battlefield predicts stock Magda wins, 61/106 vs 5/86. This is confounded (it mostly means Magda came online), so it is not Portal's causal lift.
- Finisher regex (unverified): 72/363 value-8 targets are in no line (Pact of Negation in 17/32 decks, Final Fortune, Ragavan). Pact of Negation fetched 33 times, 18 never cast. Plan seats lose to their own spell 8/256 games vs stock 1/768.
- Engine tier missing (unverified): early (R1-3) tutors fetched engines 9/18 times for humans, 0/175 for plan seats, 9% for stock. Rhystic Study, Mystic Remora, Esper Sentinel, Necropotence and Seedborn Muse are value 1 in every deck that runs them.
- Correction: about 3 in 4 of the 'non-win' steers go to real routes whose outlet is in the deck but that the Forge pilot cannot execute. Fetch class alone barely moves conversion (win-line piece 17% vs non-win piece 15% win within 2 rounds). So the fix is to value pieces by pilotability, not to demote engines wholesale.
- plan_feedback.py:196-205 raises finisher-hinted targets by 2 whenever a deck's wins are 70%+ in the combat/life bucket. That is essentially every deck, so once it has data the only learning loop pushes tutors further from combo pieces (store empty locally; production unchecked).

**Already tried.** tasks/20 Stages 0-2 (shim 0.5.0, 2026-08-24..26) added plan-driven search steering. The Portal override was accepted as 'Both are defensible' (tasks/20:139-141). Stage 2 shipped although mean win round moved the wrong way (11.75 -> 13.29). plan_feedback (08-30) added the combat-majority nudge.

**Leverage.** Data (Python) plus a data flag read by an existing shim branch. Value pieces by line executability and measured conversion lift from our own logs. Keep a finisher tier for proven closers (Portal-class cards). Restrict combo-mode steer priority to executable win lines via a per-line flag. Add an engine/opener tier that decays after round 3. Drop self-loss and saboteur text from the finisher regex. Disable plan_feedback's combat nudge until the win-method label is fixed.

**Unknowns.** No Magda A/B isolates the override from other plan-seat behaviour. Generality: every Portal override is a Magda deck; of the other 7 decks, 2 gain significantly and none loses significantly. The n7 pod's other decks (rog_ishai, selvala) still lose about 11 pp with lines off, for unexplained reasons (plan-mode steering, mulligan and holds all stay on in nocombo).

### 5. The shim's own tutor-cast decision has no model of reach, zone or mode, and fires only when a line is exactly one piece short (confidence: high)

**Symptoms.**
- tutoring: most deliberate combo tutors are spent on a false premise; the piece is often unreachable by type, already in the graveyard, or the tutor never searches at all
- tutoring: tutors act as closers, because the only trigger is 'one piece short'
- combos: commander-as-tutor opens line pursuit from turn 0 for Magda and Godo and casts pieces early

**Mechanism.** lineOfSight treats any plan tutor in hand, or a commander in the command zone (PlanPlayerController.java:1051-1055), as able to fetch any missing piece from any zone (:1063-1070). tutor_cast then casts the first castable plan tutor (:1159-1176) with no check that it can reach the piece. castableSpell hands Forge a raw SpellAbility (:1249-1262), which skips the AI's own target and X choice and casts transmute cards as their spell half. deck_plan.py:113-117 says outright that the tutor list is 'knowingly a little optimistic'.

**Evidence.**
- Against Forge's own ChangeType restrictions, 316/718 tutor_cast events (44.0%) used a tutor that cannot fetch the sought piece. Forge confirms it: 221 of 222 such casts that resolved a search did not offer the piece.
- 285/498 (57%) resolved tutor_cast searches could not deliver the piece; 98 casts sought a piece already in the graveyard or exile.
- Mode and X failures: 26 casts never reached the stack ('failed to target': Dizzy Spell and Muddle the Mixture cast as spells; Intuition and Gifts with no opponent target). All 18 shim casts of X-cost tutors (Chord, GSZ, Wargate) resolved at X=0, against 17/307 X=0 for stock casts of the same cards.
- Commander gate: Magda's command-zone presence opened the gate in 32 of 37 Magda games that were open by turn 4, and was needed for 15/30 Magda combo_casts. Imperial Recruiter and Tezzeret, Cruel Captain were cast 'seeking Helm of the Host' 13 times; neither can fetch it.
- Gate shape, corrected for commanders: among plan-seat turns ending with a tutor in hand, 36-41% are two pieces short (55% in rubric_ship) and 42-54% are one short with the gate open but no tutor cast. Decline reasons are not logged; 237/502 eligible turns had a legal, land-affordable tutor that stayed uncast (unverified; tutor keep-weight 5 ties 17-37 cards, strict inequality at :1167-1168).
- Scope: tutor_cast decides 419/858 (49%) of plan-seat tutor casts in the cEDH arms, and 0-1 casts per 768 precon games.

**Already tried.** The A18 gate (02e4451, 2026-08-24) is unchanged since. tasks/20 changed what searches pick, not when or whether to cast. Every arm from 0.5.0 to 0.16.0 ran identical tutor-cast logic.

**Leverage.** Data plus a thin shim check. deck_plan emits per-tutor reach (types, MV/power/colour caps, destination, mode: spell, ETB, activated, transmute), or the shim tests the piece against the tutor's ChangeZone valid-type through public API. Pieces outside the library count as unreachable. Let Forge's AI finish X and targets, or skip such tutors. Tie commander tutor status to its zone. Log the reason for each decline. Only after reach works, add an opener window as a data dial (for example tutorOpenDistance, or an engine target worth more than the stock pick).

**Unknowns.** Why eligible tutors go uncast (weight tie, mana or timing) cannot be told from logs. The win-rate effect of fixing reach is unmeasured. Stock-seat fetch attribution is inferred from zone moves.

### 6. The research system could not see the combo and tutor failures, so two months of work went to combat and timing (confidence: high)

**Symptoms.**
- combos, tutoring and play_logic all persisted because every study was judged on metrics that are blind to them
- measurement: 'parity with stock' was read as 'the agent does nothing', when it was +18 pp on Oracle decks and -7 pp on the rest

**Mechanism.** Every acceptance gate was pooled win share against stock, correlation with precon humans, behaviour-marker rates or mechanism self-checks. Most games ran on precons, where the combo and tutor code essentially never fired (partly because of a parse bug). No study measured conversion, win round against humans, win method, or whether Forge was willing to play the cards the plan needed. The project correctly concluded on 08-24/08-29 that the gap is conversion and strategy content, then built combat and timing features for the next month.

**Evidence.**
- 0 of about 10 acceptance gates (tasks 07, 20, 21, 23, engine A/B) measure conversion or win round. The rubric's 76 criteria cover blocking, attacking, interaction and mulligans only.
- Pooled parity hides opposite effects: Oracle decks plan 20/96 vs stock 9/288 (+17.7 pp, +4.1 SE); the other six decks 64/288 vs 251/864 (-6.8 pp).
- Population: on-disk study corpus 6,135 games; combo- or tutor-focused runs are 452-708 (7-12%). Precon agent arms logged combo_cast 0-4 and tutor_cast 0-1 per 768 games; the 1,536-game overnight ablation logged 2 combo casts and 1 tutor cast.
- The only lever that moved win speed toward humans was combo pursuit: 15/93 plan wins by round 6 vs 6/308 stock (p about 2e-6), almost all Oracle. The study read it as a 3.2 pp cost and nearly dropped it.
- After 08-29: comboPriority, lineOfSight and tutor_cast untouched; combos.py untouched since 07-31; none of the 1,289 study games run after 08-29 came from a combo or tutor study. Shipped work was 0.10.1 re-aim, 0.14.0 attack targeting, 0.15.0 instant hold and the 0.16.0 combat solver, prompted by the precon backlog, replay callouts and an outside note.
- No study measured Forge-side refusals: RemoveDeck, the MAIN2 tutor gate, KeyCards, and setUseSimulation have 0 mentions in the repo.
- The human decision corpus was never built: 12 cEDH video games with about 12 expert-labelled decisions, plus 6 midrange tallies. The planned self-recorded capture never happened.

**Already tried.** Nine or more arms (personality dials, hold-back, blockiness, synergy lines, split attacks, 0.15.0 hold, 0.16.0 solver) and the 1,536-game overnight run were all judged on pooled win share or precon correlation. The conversion design was named twice and never tasked.

**Leverage.** Product and reporting (Python/harness, no boundary issue). Adopt a combo scorecard for every AI change on combo-live pods: conversion rate by line class, share of wins by round 6 and mean win round against the human 5.0, non-combat combo wins, tutor timing and targets, Forge-refusal rates (flagged cast/activation rate, tutor phase, tutor pick type). Report per deck, stratified by line executability. Build a seeded-board scenario harness. Keep precons for prediction only. Freeze combat work.

**Unknowns.** The 12 human games are creator-selected (biased toward fast, exciting games) and several decklists drift, so the true human distribution is unknown. Whether the owner can record 20-30 labelled games is unknown.

### 7. Forge's own tutor AI waits for main phase 2, grabs the best creature, and the data hooks Forge provides to change that are unused (confidence: high)

**Symptoms.**
- tutoring: stock tutors are cast in main phase 2, where most fetched cards cannot be used that turn; upkeep and in-response tutoring (the human pattern) does not happen
- tutoring: generic tutors fetch Spirit Guides and big creatures instead of the deck's key cards

**Mechanism.** ChangeZoneAi.hiddenOriginCanPlayAI refuses a library-to-hand tutor before MAIN2 unless it is the only card in hand, and refuses library-to-library tutors (Vampiric, Mystical, Worldly, Enlightened, Imperial Seal) before MAIN2 on any turn. Only an ActivationPhases param or AILogic AnyMainPhase changes this, and only Karn the Great Creator sets AnyMainPhase. With no KeyCards set, the chooser falls back to the best creature. Forge has no profile key or AILogic about combos or tutoring, so personality dials cannot reach any of this.

**Evidence.**

### 8. The one line that converts (Consultation + Thassa's Oracle) works through an unintended stack check and also decks its own pilot (confidence: high)

**Symptoms.**
- combos: the only human-speed wins in the whole corpus come from this line, and it self-decks about once for every two wins
- play_logic: Thassa's Oracle is cast as a bare creature 53 of 92 times, burning the win condition

**Mechanism.** comboPriority's guard `if (!getGame().getStackZone().isEmpty()) return stock;` (PlanPlayerController.java:1094) checks the zone that holds spell cards, not MagicStack, where triggers live. So the shim can cast Consultation while Oracle's ETB trigger is pending, which is the correct human line, even though the commit meant 'only on an empty stack'. The line model has no order and no notion of a spent trigger. The shim casts Oracle early as a 'developing permanent' (:1136), passes stock's own Oracle casts through (:1107), and later counts an Oracle already on the battlefield as a live piece. It then fires Consultation into an empty trigger window and exiles its own library.

**Evidence.**
- Oracle wins: 25, all plan seats, 0 by stock (stock never casts the flagged Consultation). Median round 6.5 across all 8 run sets (5.0 in the 20-win subset) vs 11.5 for all other wins.
- 49 plan Consultation casts: 31 in response to the Oracle trigger (25 won, 3 failed because stock Forge named a card still in the library, 2 countered); 15 after the trigger resolved (12 decked); 3 into an opponent's Torpor Orb (2 decked). 14 self-decks in total.
- 53 of 92 plan Oracle casts spent the ETB with no win attempt: 13 chosen by the shim's combo_cast, 40 stock picks let through at :1107.
- Example: runs_015_default cell_2iA_Jt0d6sM_rot3 g3, seq 279 Oracle cast, 281 trigger, 283 Consultation, 293 win. Same file g11: Oracle t17, Consultation t21, lost to an empty library.
- MagicStack bytecode: triggered abilities go only onto the Deque; getStackZone() receives spell cards only.

**Already tried.** Stage 5's 'completes' path (54eb2f6) and the 0.9.3 veto. Nothing has addressed ordering or spent triggers.

**Leverage.** Data plus a thin shim rule. A per-piece fire-last flag and zone from Spellbook's zoneLocations; hold Oracle until the exiler is castable in the same window; never count a spent-ETB Oracle as online; check for static suppressors (Torpor Orb); steer Consultation's card name (chooseCardName is not overridden). Warning: switching :1094 to getStack().isEmpty() alone would close the only winning window and turn this line into a guaranteed self-deck.

**Unknowns.** Whether the trigger is pending (not yet on the stack) when the shim picks Consultation is inferred from log sequence numbers, not Forge source. The line exists in about 5-8 cEDH decks, so the win-rate ceiling of a fix is narrow.
